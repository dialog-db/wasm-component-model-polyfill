## What to build
The last feature, sequenced last on purpose: the stackful form of `canon lift async` (no `callback`), and a synchronous guest calling an asynchronous host function. Both need the guest's live stack suspended. The design must state, per target, what is available and what the polyfill does: JSPI in the browser through the vendored backend, with the browsers that ship it named from the compatibility outlook; natively, what `wasm_runtime_layer` can offer (an upstream proposal if a fiber-based call is needed) and whether a block-on fallback is ever safe; and asyncify as a route that rewrites the guest, with its costs. It must state the conformance ceiling on a target without a stack switch, the availability risk of a blocked call that cannot suspend and how it is bounded, and the guest toolchains that need this form (those with their own scheduler) versus those that do not (wit-bindgen's Rust guests use the callback form).

The thread built-ins land here too: `thread.index`, `thread.new-indirect` (with the `ExtractTable` initializer the translator already emits), `thread.resume-later`, `thread.suspend`, `thread.suspend-then-resume`, `thread.yield-then-resume`, `thread.suspend-then-promote`, and `thread.yield-then-promote`. Every one of them needs a second guest thread inside one task, and all but `thread.index`, `thread.new-indirect`, and `thread.resume-later` suspend or switch the running stack, so they need the same suspend seam as the stackful lift. The tasks-and-callback-export design leaves them unsupported at translation. They gate the second directive of `task-return-traps.wast`, `self-switch-traps.wast`, `join-during-sync-read.wast`, the `during-sync-*.wast` group, and the thread cases of `reentrance.wast`, `switch-to-ready-callback.wast`, `trap-if-block-and-sync.wast`, and `task-deletion.wast`.

This card is blocked by the streams and futures design because a stackful task blocks on the same waitables; it is sequenced after the error-context and cancellation design by priority, not by dependency.

Two facts of the seam as it stands, found by the checkup of 2026-09-21, are input to this design. `SuspendSeam::suspend_with_provider` (`src/concurrency/suspend_seam.rs:232-239`) takes the provider out of its slot for the length of the suspension, so a block reached from guest work the provider's condition check runs finds the slot empty and takes the nested-turn fallback, which contradicts the seam's own doc at lines 209-214 that the two never meet; the design must say which is right. And in the browser a nested turn cannot enter any host function already on the stack, including the blocking built-in's own trampoline, so two tasks of one instance blocking in the same built-in fail there and pass natively; the provider is what removes that difference, and the design should name it as such.

Test Cases: a stackful export that blocks and resumes on a browser with JSPI; the documented outcome on a browser without it; a synchronous export calling an async host function on each target; the corpus files that only pass with a stack switch, listed per target.

Added 2026-09-23 from the streams-and-futures work (card ad5dd3), pending its reviewer's confirmation: `stream-zero-ops.wast:201` (a sync-lifted callee reached through an async lower blocks in `waitable-set.wait` until its caller writes) and `streams-massive-send.wast` (a sync write after `task.return` in a callee reached by a sync lower, read by the caller below) deadlock without a stack switch, like `sync-streams.wast`. Also on this list since: seven lines of `wasmtime/async/trap-if-done.wast` and `task-builtins.wast:723` (a callee synchronously reads a future only its caller writes), both accepted by the owner on 2026-09-25, and the live run of the closing corpus audit also puts `cancel-and-exclusive-lock.wast:196` and `reenter-during-yield.wast:81` behind a stack switch. The owner asked that each such corpus line name this milestone, and that this design list them among the files that pass once the suspend capability has a provider.
